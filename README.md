### 2025 Retail Sales Analysis
**Tools Used:** Excel · SQL · Tableau

## Executive Summary

### Objective

This project pulls together transaction, product, customer, discount, and inventory data to answer specific questions about how the business performed in 2025 — where sales and profit came from, how customers behaved, which products and discounts performed well, and how inventory moved.

The goal was to identify what was driving performance, flag potential risks, and turn the analysis into recommendations management could act on.

## Business Questions

The project was developed around a defined set of business questions covering profitability, customer behavior, sales consistency, discount effectiveness, inventory management, and performance trends.

These questions guided the SQL analysis and subsequent Tableau dashboard development.

1. Which departments generate the highest sales and profit, and which have the strongest profit margins?

2. Which department generates the most consistent sales, and which has the greatest volatility?

3. Which products and product categories contribute most to overall revenue and profitability?

4. How do customer segments differ in purchasing behavior, spending, and profitability?

5. How do discounts affect sales volume, revenue, and profit margins?

6. What actionable opportunities can management identify to improve revenue, profitability, and inventory efficiency?

---

## Data & Methodology

Transaction-level retail data was analyzed to evaluate sales, profitability, customer behavior, product performance, discounts, inventory, and performance trends.

**Excel** was used during the initial data preparation stage to clean and organize the datasets. XLOOKUP was used to match product information with transaction records, while PivotTables were used to validate totals and explore preliminary trends.

The cleaned data was then imported into **SQL**, where the analysis included profitability calculations, customer segmentation, product analysis, monthly sales trends, and sales consistency.

Departmental sales stability was measured using the **coefficient of variation (CV)** to distinguish consistent performers from more volatile departments.

The final analytical results were visualized in **Tableau** through interactive dashboards designed to support management decision-making.

# Key Findings

### Department Performance

The **Women's department** generated the strongest overall sales and profit, producing **$758,423.21 in sales** and **$357,187.87 in profit**.

While Women's generated the most sales, **Kids had the highest profit margin at 47.2%**, compared with approximately 47.1% for Women's and 46.6% for Men's.

---

### Sales Consistency & Volatility

The **Men's department** generated the most consistent monthly sales, with a coefficient of variation of approximately **5.24%**.

Women's followed at approximately **5.86%**, while Kids showed the greatest monthly volatility at approximately **9.97%**.

This suggests that Kids sales were considerably more variable throughout the year and may warrant additional investigation into seasonality, product mix, promotions, or inventory timing.

---

### Product Profitability

The **top five products generated approximately 37.11% of total company profit**, demonstrating that profitability was concentrated among a relatively small number of products.

The **Leather Loafer generated approximately $63,700 in profit**, making it the strongest individual profit contributor identified in the analysis.

---

### Customer Analysis

**Loyalty customers** represented the largest customer segment:

- 6,231 transactions
- $649,309.51 in sales
- $304,814.08 in profit
- $104.21 average transaction value

Returning customers generated **$461,177.22 in sales**, while new customers generated **$436,679.43**.

Despite differences in transaction volume and spending, profit margins remained extremely similar across all three customer groups at approximately **46.94%–47.00%**.

---

### Discount Analysis

Profit margins remained around **47% across the different discount groups**, with no meaningful improvement in profitability as discount levels increased.

The results suggest that heavier discounting did not produce a clear margin advantage and should be evaluated based on its effect on sales volume and revenue rather than assumed to improve profitability.

---

# Recommendations

- **Prioritize inventory availability for top-profit products**, beginning with the Leather Loafer and the remainder of the top-five profit contributors. Because these products account for 37.11% of total profit, stockouts could put meaningful profit at risk.

- **Investigate the Kids department's higher sales variability** to determine whether seasonality, product mix, promotions, or inventory timing is driving the larger monthly swings.

- **Reevaluate discount strategy at the product and transaction level.** Margins remained near 47% regardless of discount depth, showing no clear profitability lift from heavier discounting.

- **Develop customer strategies by segment.** Loyalty, Returning, and New customers produced similar margins but differed in transaction volume and spending behavior.

- **Evaluate profitability and inventory movement alongside sales** when determining replenishment priorities and identifying potential overstock risks.
